More than a reset timer

See the capacity you use over time.

Find out how many tokens you have burned since your last reset, compare Codex and Claude on the same clock, and keep the whole record on your PC.

Compare

Codex and Claude, on one clock.

The two providers reset on different schedules, so Ceiling lines them up on rolling windows that end right now. That is the only way the comparison is fair.

  • See which assistant is doing more work, over the last five hours and the last seven days.
  • Reset-aligned figures stay in each provider's own view, where they belong.
  • Read it without exporting anything to a third-party dashboard.

Since your last reset

Know what this window has cost you.

Every provider window shows the tokens processed since it reset, with an estimated API value beside them. That figure is an API-equivalent estimate from your local logs, not a subscription bill.

  • Tokens and estimated value since your 5-hour and weekly resets.
  • Plus a 30-day breakdown by model, reasoning effort, and project.
  • Models with no public price stay excluded rather than reading as $0.00.

When you work

Find the hours you actually build in.

Ceiling already has the timestamps, so it can answer a question no provider dashboard will: not how much you used, but when. A 30-day strip and a weekday-by-hour grid, drawn from logs already on your disk.

  • See your real working week, including the parts you would not have guessed.
  • Read it in tokens or in estimated API value, for Codex, Claude, and Grok, one at a time or together.
  • Nothing new is collected and nothing leaves the machine.

Always nearby

Useful when you are working, quiet when you are not.

The capacity strip sits above the taskbar without stealing focus. Hover for a fuller view, open the tray flyout when you need detail, and stay out of a dashboard the rest of the time.

Optional alerts can flag a limit approaching, an unexpected reset, restored capacity, or a banked reset.

Local means local

Ceiling does not run a server for your usage data.

It reads usage from sources on your PC or each provider’s own usage endpoint. Credentials, cookies you paste, and API keys are opt-in and remain on your machine.
